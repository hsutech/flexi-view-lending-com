Best Hospitality Financing Lenders: 7 Options to Compare

Hotel, motel and lodging deals are underwritten differently from ordinary commercial real estate. This guide compares hospitality financing options by use case, documentation and execution risk instead of pretending one lender is best for every property.

Quick answer

The best hospitality financing lender is the one whose credit box matches the hotel today: stabilized acquisition, flagged refinance, PIP renovation, turnaround bridge, SBA owner-operator purchase, ground-up construction or portfolio recapitalization. Compare lenders by hotel specialization, eligible property type, leverage, timing, franchise requirements, PIP treatment, cash-flow underwriting and exit plan.

Current Google US and California results for "best hospitality financing lenders" are mostly lender product pages, hotel-loan brokers, commercial real estate finance pages and 2026 hotel financing explainers. The strongest pages explain why hotel loans are specialized, list available programs such as conventional, SBA, bridge, construction and CMBS, and discuss metrics like occupancy, average daily rate, RevPAR, trailing financials, brand strength and property improvement plans.

The gap is that many pages promote a single lender or program without helping borrowers decide whether their property belongs in permanent debt, bridge debt, SBA financing or construction capital. Hospitality assets sit between real estate and an operating business. A borrower comparing lenders should therefore compare product fit first, then pricing, then closing certainty.

Seven hospitality financing lenders and paths to compare

1. Wells Fargo Hospitality Finance Group for larger hotel sponsors

Wells Fargo's hospitality property financing page is one of the clearest large-bank examples in the SERP. It describes hospitality finance for leading hotel owners and operators in the United States, including institutional investors, public hotel companies, private equity funds and well-capitalized private developers or operators.

This option is most relevant when the borrower has a larger, more institutional file: stabilized lodging assets, portfolios, substantial rehabilitation, construction, bridge or mini-perm needs. Wells Fargo also lists recourse and non-recourse financing, lines of credit and floating-rate facilities among its hospitality finance capabilities. For smaller or transitional hotel borrowers, the practical question is whether the request meets the bank's relationship, sponsorship and asset-quality expectations before spending time on a bank process.

2. HotelLoans.com for hotel-specific program matching

HotelLoans.com is built around the hotel financing search itself. Its site presents conventional, SBA, construction and bridge loan programs, and it says it works as a direct lender for hospitality properties while also partnering with other lenders to make programs available for building, buying or refinancing hotel property.

That can be useful when a borrower is still deciding whether the file belongs in conventional debt, SBA financing, construction capital or a bridge loan. The diligence is role clarity. Ask who is funding the loan, whether the company is acting as a lender, broker or correspondent on your transaction, whether any upfront fee applies, what program actually fits the asset, and what documents are needed before a term sheet is meaningful.

3. Lumen Mortgage for RevPAR-based hospitality underwriting

Lumen Mortgage's hotel and hospitality page emphasizes RevPAR, ADR and STR report underwriting for hotel and hospitality financing. That matters because hotels are not underwritten like simple leased commercial buildings. Room revenue, seasonality, competitive set and brand or management quality can change the credit picture quickly.

Lumen may be worth comparing when the property has a story that needs hospitality-specific operating metrics rather than only a real estate appraisal. The borrower should still verify loan amount, state availability, NMLS or licensing status where applicable, whether the company brokers or funds the debt, how it treats independent boutique properties versus flagged hotels, and whether projections are accepted or only trailing performance is used.

4. First National for direct hospitality commercial real estate lending

First National's hospitality financing page describes direct commercial real estate lending for acquisition, refinance, renovation, construction and expansion of hotels and hospitality properties. The page also separates property types such as full-service hotels, limited-service hotels, boutique properties, resorts, flagged and independent assets, motels and extended-stay properties.

This can fit borrowers who want a lender that understands both asset fundamentals and hotel revenue stability. The key comparison points are servicing, draw controls for renovations, whether the lender will hold the loan, the required operating history, franchise or PIP conditions, reserves, sponsor liquidity and how quickly the lender can move from preliminary terms to credit approval.

5. Commercial Capital Partners for multiple hotel capital routes

Commercial Capital Partners presents hotel and hospitality financing across SBA 7(a), SBA 504, conventional, CMBS, bridge, mezzanine and construction options. That broad menu reflects the real shape of the SERP: many hotel borrowers are not only choosing a lender, they are choosing the capital route.

This path may help when a flagged hotel, independent motel, brand-required renovation or ground-up project could qualify under several structures. The benefit is route comparison. The risk is that broad access can sound simpler than execution. Confirm which lender would actually fund the loan, whether the borrower pays any fee, how the advisor is compensated, whether the proposed route fits the closing deadline, and what happens if franchise approval or PIP scope changes.

6. SBA 504 or SBA 7(a) lender path for eligible owner-operators

For some hotel and motel borrowers, the best comparison is not a private lender versus another private lender. It is whether an SBA structure fits at all. The SBA's 504 loan page explains that 504 loans are available through Certified Development Companies and can be used for major fixed assets such as existing facilities, while also listing uses that are not permitted. SBA 7(a) lenders may also finance eligible business acquisition, real estate and working-capital needs depending on the file.

SBA financing can be attractive for eligible owner-operated hospitality scenarios, but it is document-heavy and rule-driven. Verify owner-occupancy and use-of-proceeds requirements, whether the hotel is an operating business rather than passive rental real estate, borrower contribution, timing, environmental review, franchise documents, PIP funding, CDC involvement for 504 loans and whether the lender has hospitality experience.

7. Flexi View Lending for scenario routing before the file is boxed

Flexi View Lending is relevant when a borrower needs to decide whether a hospitality request belongs in a hospitality loan, bridge loan, construction loan, refinance loan or business loan. A hotel deal can move between those categories depending on operations, collateral, timeline and use of funds.

The most useful first package includes the property address, purchase contract or payoff statement, trailing twelve-month operating statement, occupancy and ADR history, PIP estimate if applicable, franchise agreement or brand status, property photos, borrower experience, requested amount, use of funds and exit plan. Borrowers can start with the application page when the basic facts are ready for review.

How to compare hospitality financing lenders

Start with the property status. A stabilized flagged hotel with clean trailing financials may fit permanent bank, CMBS or life-company style debt. A property with deferred maintenance, a brand-mandated PIP, weak occupancy or a short closing deadline may need bridge capital first. A ground-up hotel project depends on cost budget, permits, sponsor strength, completion risk and takeout financing. A smaller owner-operated motel may belong in an SBA conversation if eligibility and timing fit.

Next, compare the underwriting lens. Hospitality lenders usually look at occupancy, average daily rate, revenue per available room, trailing operating income, franchise strength, management, market demand, seasonality, PIP cost, reserves, borrower liquidity and prior hotel experience. A lender that is comfortable with apartments or retail may still be uncomfortable with hotels. Ask directly how many similar hotel loans the lender has closed and what usually causes files to fail.

Finally, model the delay case. Hotel financing often depends on third parties: appraisers, environmental consultants, franchise approvals, PIP estimates, SBA or CDC review, title, insurance, zoning and sometimes liquor, restaurant or parking issues. Compare extension rights, deposits, minimum interest, inspection requirements, reserve holdbacks, prepayment treatment, recourse and default interest before choosing the fastest-looking option.

  • Match the lender to the current asset, not only the future business plan.
  • Ask whether the quoted terms are preliminary, credit-approved or subject to capital partner approval.
  • Confirm how PIP, FF&E, working capital and renovation funds are treated.
  • Check whether projected performance can be used or trailing financials control the loan amount.
  • Verify license, broker role, funding source and fee treatment before paying deposits.

California diligence before choosing a hospitality lender

California borrowers should verify who they are dealing with before relying on a term sheet. The California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation explains that the California Financing Law covers licensing and regulation of finance lenders and brokers making or brokering consumer and commercial loans, subject to exceptions. The California Department of Real Estate's multi-jurisdiction lookup is also a useful starting point for real estate and financial services license checks.

Licensing does not prove a loan is suitable, and exemptions may apply. It does help confirm identity and reduce avoidable risk. Borrowers should ask whether the party is a direct lender, broker, correspondent, bank, SBA lender, CDC, debt fund or advisor, and should avoid wiring money based only on verbal approval. Written terms, documented authority and a realistic path to closing matter more than urgency.

FAQs

What is hospitality financing?

Hospitality financing is commercial financing for hotels, motels, resorts and other lodging properties. It can include acquisition, refinance, renovation, construction, bridge, SBA, CMBS, conventional bank and working-capital structures.

Who are the best hospitality financing lenders?

There is no single best lender for every hotel. The right option depends on loan size, property type, flag, occupancy, trailing income, borrower experience, timing, PIP scope and whether the deal needs permanent or temporary capital.

What do hotel lenders evaluate?

Hotel lenders commonly evaluate trailing operating statements, occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, franchise status, PIP obligations, market position, management, borrower liquidity, experience, valuation, reserves and exit strategy.

Can SBA loans finance hotels?

SBA financing can be relevant for eligible owner-operated hotels and motels, but program rules matter. Borrowers should verify eligibility, permitted use of proceeds, timing and lender requirements with qualified SBA lenders or Certified Development Companies.

Is a bridge loan better than permanent hotel financing?

Not automatically. A bridge loan may fit a value-add acquisition, deadline, renovation or stabilization period. Permanent debt may be cleaner for a stabilized property with strong cash flow. The right choice depends on the current problem and exit plan.

What documents should I prepare before requesting hotel loan terms?

Prepare the purchase contract or payoff statement, trailing financials, occupancy and ADR history, STR or competitive set context if available, PIP estimate, franchise documents, property photos, borrower financials, use of funds and exit plan.

Final thought

Hospitality financing works best when the lender understands both the real estate and the hotel operation. Before comparing advertised rates, compare the lender's fit with the property's current condition, brand status, operating history, PIP needs and exit. That order gives borrowers a better chance of choosing capital that can actually close.
