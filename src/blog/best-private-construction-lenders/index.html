Best Private Construction Lenders: 7 Options to Compare

Private construction financing is not just a fast loan. It is a draw-based capital structure tied to plans, permits, builder performance, cost controls and a credible exit.

Quick answer

The best private construction lender is the lender whose draw process, leverage, builder approval, cost-overrun policy and exit strategy match the actual project. A single infill SFR in Los Angeles, a build-to-rent plan, a heavy rehab that crosses into construction, and a portfolio of spec builds can each point to a different lender.

Current Google US results for this keyword lean commercial and BOFU. The top relevant pages are lender listicles, private lender comparison pages and direct-lender product pages. They usually include comparison tables, updated dates, program details, lender-by-scenario recommendations, FAQs and calls to apply. Forbes Advisor covers consumer-oriented construction lenders, while investor-focused pages from LYNK Mortgage, Kiavi, Lima One Capital, RCN Capital, CoreVest and other private lenders focus on ground-up, infill, draw schedules, LTC, ARV or after-completion value, and build-to-rent exits.

This guide follows that intent without ranking any company as universally best. Construction loans are too dependent on borrower experience, builder readiness, permit status, budget realism, collateral, local market depth, reserves and whether the exit is sale or refinance. Use the options below as a shortlist for questions, not as a substitute for underwriting.

Seven private construction lender options for investors

1. Kiavi for infill new construction with a streamlined digital process

Kiavi publishes a dedicated new construction and infill loan page for real estate developers. Its public materials emphasize property-based pricing, a digital process, single or dual close options, build-to-sell and build-to-rent strategies, and loan sizing tied to total project cost and ARV. Kiavi also states that its construction loans are for investment properties and lists California among available states.

That makes Kiavi a logical candidate for a plan-ready residential infill project where speed and an online workflow matter. Investors still need to check the fine print. Ask what "permit-ready" means for the specific city, whether closing can occur before the permit is issued, how draws are requested, how extensions work and whether the valuation model supports the completed value you are underwriting.

2. Lima One Capital for builders comparing construction and rental exits

Lima One Capital appears in construction lender searches because it publishes a detailed new construction program for investors. Its page describes vertical construction financing for infill, shovel-ready and entitled land, plus Build2Rent and bridge-related paths. Lima One also discusses project documentation such as plans, line-item budgets, borrower underwriting and general contractor review.

This is especially relevant when the exit could change. A builder may plan to sell a completed home but later hold it as a rental if resale conditions soften. Before relying on that flexibility, confirm whether the construction loan and the long-term takeout are separately underwritten, whether a second closing is needed, and what reserves or lease-up assumptions apply after completion.

3. RCN Capital for broker-accessible construction and investor programs

RCN Capital publishes loan programs for real estate investors that include fix-and-flip, bridge, rental portfolio, multifamily and new construction. The current SERP also surfaces RCN through comparison pages that describe it as a common option for construction and broker-originated investor lending.

RCN can be worth comparing when a borrower or broker wants one channel to evaluate construction against other short-term or long-term investor products. The key is to identify the exact lane. A ground-up construction loan is not the same as a heavy rehab loan, and both differ from a DSCR rental refinance. Ask whether the quote is direct or brokered, how builder approval works, how draws are controlled and what completed-project experience is assumed.

4. CoreVest for larger builders and credit-line style capital

CoreVest positions its products around real estate investors, including rental loans, bridge loans, credit lines, ground-up new construction, build-for-rent and multifamily loans. Its public product page also lists California offices and California Finance Lenders license information, which is useful context for diligence.

CoreVest is more relevant to experienced operators than to a first speculative build. A builder running several SFR projects or a build-for-rent strategy may care about credit-line capacity, repeatable underwriting and portfolio controls. Review minimum loan size, reserves, reporting, recourse, geographic constraints and whether the project is better treated as ground-up construction, bridge, multifamily or a rental product.

5. Temple View Capital for brokered ground-up programs

Temple View Capital publishes a ground-up construction program through TVC Funding. Its page is oriented toward brokers and experienced builders, with program language around new builds, extensive reconstruction, 1-4 unit properties, entity borrowers and short-term terms that are subject to underwriting.

This type of lender may fit borrowers who already work through a mortgage broker or private lending advisor. It may not fit a consumer owner-builder or a borrower expecting bank-style construction-to-permanent financing. Confirm borrower entity requirements, current program terms, escrow or draw rules, licensing, who communicates with the builder and whether the quote came from a wholesale channel or a direct retail channel.

6. LYNK Mortgage for one relationship across construction and DSCR

LYNK Mortgage ranks with a detailed construction lender comparison page that also promotes its own construction, bridge, fix-and-flip and DSCR products. The page correctly highlights a major difference between construction lending and ordinary bridge loans: construction capital is usually released in milestone-based draws rather than as one lump sum.

LYNK may be worth comparing when the investor wants construction debt and a possible DSCR rental exit under one relationship. Because its page includes marketing claims and published sample terms, borrowers should verify what is current for the property state, deal structure and borrower experience. Also ask whether the lender is licensed, exempt or otherwise authorized for the transaction type being proposed.

7. Local or brokered private capital for California-specific files

Some California construction loans need more explanation than a national intake form can capture. Examples include hillside lots, ADU-heavy projects, small multifamily conversions, mixed-use properties, delayed permits, fire rebuilds, environmental concerns, partially completed projects or borrowers with strong builder experience but limited financial documentation.

In those cases, local or brokered private capital can be valuable if it organizes the file honestly. A company such as Flexi View Lending can help investors frame whether the request belongs in construction loans, bridge loans, hard money loans or fix and flip loans before a lender reviews documents. Borrowers should still ask who funds the loan, who gets paid, whether a term sheet is binding and who services the loan after closing.

How to compare private construction lenders

Start with project fit. A private construction lender underwrites more than credit. The file usually needs a land or purchase story, plans, permit status, a line-item budget, soft costs, contingency, builder credentials, insurance, title, entity documents, comparable sales or rental assumptions, and a realistic exit. If those pieces are weak, speed claims do not matter.

Next, model loan sizing both ways. Construction lenders often quote loan-to-cost and loan-to-value, after-repair value or after-completion value. The binding limit is typically whichever calculation produces the lower loan amount. A loan advertised at a high LTC can still require more borrower cash if the completed value is not strong enough.

Then inspect the draw process. Draws determine whether the project has oxygen after closing. Ask how many draws are typical, what milestones release funds, who inspects, who pays inspection or fund-control fees, how quickly wires are sent, whether contingency is held back and whether interest is charged only on drawn funds or on the full commitment.

Finally, pressure-test the exit. Spec builders need a sale plan backed by realistic comparable sales. Build-to-rent investors need a refinance plan that accounts for DSCR, lease-up, taxes, insurance and valuation. Every borrower should ask about extension fees, default interest, maturity notices and what happens if permits, utilities, materials or inspections delay completion.

  • Compare total cash to close, not just interest rate.
  • Ask whether the builder must be approved before closing.
  • Confirm if the lender funds horizontal work, vertical work or both.
  • Review cost-overrun and contingency rules before signing.
  • Get draw, extension and servicing terms in writing.

California diligence before choosing a lender

California licensing analysis depends on the loan purpose, collateral, borrower, broker role and exemptions. The California Financing Law resources from the DFPI state that the law licenses and regulates finance lenders and brokers making or brokering consumer and commercial loans, except as specified. California Financial Code section 22100 also says a person generally may not engage in the business of a finance lender or broker without a license from the commissioner unless an exception applies.

That does not mean every private construction loan uses the same license. Some real estate-secured transactions involve a DRE broker, a finance lender license, a residential mortgage lending framework, an exemption or a business-purpose structure. The practical step is simple: verify the party making or arranging the loan. Use the California Department of Real Estate public license lookup, NMLS where applicable and written disclosures from the lender or broker.

Do not rely only on a website badge, a fast-closing claim or a verbal quote. Confirm legal name, license or exemption basis, fee deposits, refund terms, servicing contact, draw administrator and whether the person sending the term sheet has authority to bind the lender. A slower, documented yes is usually better than a fast indication that collapses after plans, permits or title are reviewed.

FAQs

Who is the best private construction lender?

There is no universal best lender. The right fit depends on project scope, location, builder experience, borrower liquidity, permit status, loan size, draw process and sale or refinance exit.

How do private construction loan draws work?

Most lenders do not wire the full construction budget at closing. Funds are released in stages after work is completed, inspected or otherwise verified. The exact process should be written into the loan documents.

What is the difference between LTC and LTV in construction lending?

Loan-to-cost measures the loan against purchase price, hard costs and sometimes soft costs. Loan-to-value or after-completion value measures the loan against finished value. The lower limit often controls the actual loan amount.

Can a first-time builder get a private construction loan?

Some lenders consider newer builders, but they usually require stronger reserves, a qualified general contractor, clear plans, realistic budget, permits or permit status, and a conservative exit.

Are private construction loans only for LLCs?

Many business-purpose construction lenders prefer or require entity borrowers such as LLCs, LPs or corporations. Requirements vary by lender, state, property use and transaction structure.

What documents should I prepare before applying?

Prepare plans, permit status, purchase contract or title information, line-item budget, builder resume, contractor license and insurance, schedule of values, comparable sales or rent support, entity documents, reserves and exit plan.

Final thought

A private construction loan should be matched to the build, not chosen from a headline rate alone. Compare lenders by draw reliability, builder approval, leverage math, contingency rules, extension policy and exit support. When the project package is organized, the application page is the cleanest way to share the facts needed for a practical review.
